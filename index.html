November 25th, 2026The five-minute version

The readings: Jeremiah spent most of his life announcing disaster. But in chapters 30–33, the "Book of Comfort," he turns. God will gather the scattered exiles "as a shepherd does a flock." They'll come home and sing on Zion. "They shall be radiant over the goodness of the Lord, over the grain, the wine, and the oil." "Their life shall become like a watered garden, and they shall never languish again." Young women will dance, and God will turn their mourning into joy (Jeremiah 31:13, paraphrased). In John, Jesus says anyone who hears his word "has passed from death to life."

What it means: On the eve of Thanksgiving, Jeremiah gives the reasons: grain, wine, oil, the young of the flock. Ordinary harvest things. Gratitude starts with seeing the goodness already on the table, and remembering how far you've been brought.

Across the traditions: the same truth, in other words.

  • Marcus Aurelius: "Adapt thyself to the things with which thy lot has been cast: and the men among whom thou hast received thy portion, love them, but do it truly, sincerely." (Meditations 6.39, Long.)
  • Judaism: Sukkot, the harvest festival, is called "the season of our joy."
  • Buddha: He called gratitude a mark of a good person (Anguttara Nikaya, paraphrased).
  • Islam: The Qur'an asks again and again, which of your Lord's blessings will you deny? (Surah 55, paraphrased.)

Carry it today: What goodness will you count tonight?

Yesterday, the good shepherd was paid off with thirty pieces of silver. Today, the Shepherd gathers his flock home to a feast.

1. He Who Scattered Will Gather

"Hear the word of the Lord, O nations, and declare it in the coastlands far away; say, 'He who scattered Israel will gather him and will keep him as a shepherd does a flock.'"

  • Tell the world: The news isn't just for Israel; the coastlands far away should hear it.
  • The same God: The one who allowed the scattering does the gathering.

2. Sing Aloud on Zion

"They shall come and sing aloud on the height of Zion, and they shall be radiant over the goodness of the Lord."

  • Radiant: The Hebrew word means to shine or stream, like light on a face.
  • Coming home: The exiles return to the place they mourned.

3. Grain, Wine and Oil

"Over the grain, the wine, and the oil, and over the young of the flock and the herd."

  • Ordinary blessings: Bread, drink, oil for lamps and skin, new lambs.
  • Thanksgiving table: The same kinds of goods we'll set out tomorrow.

4. A Watered Garden

"Their life shall become like a watered garden, and they shall never languish again."

  • Eden echo: A garden watered by rivers, like the first one.
  • Mourning into joy: God promises to turn their mourning into joy and comfort them (Jeremiah 31:13, paraphrased).

5. From Death to Life

Jesus: "Very truly, I tell you, anyone who hears my word and believes him who sent me has eternal life and does not come under judgment but has passed from death to life." Jeremiah's harvest and Jesus' promise meet: the life God gives isn't only someday. It's a garden already being watered. Gratitude is the habit of noticing that.

For Christians today

Thanksgiving has deep roots in Christian practice.

  • The oldest readings: The fathers read Jeremiah's "grain, wine and oil" as the bread, wine and anointing oil of the sacraments.
  • Catholic: Today's Gospel promises that not a hair of your head will be lost, and the Psalm sings, "Great and wonderful are all your works."
  • Episcopal: James Huntington, today's commemoration, founded the first American Anglican religious order for men and worked among the poor of New York's Lower East Side.
  • Protestant and evangelical: Many churches hold a Thanksgiving Eve service tonight, often ecumenical, giving thanks and collecting food for the hungry.
  • Eucharist: The word itself means "thanksgiving" in Greek.

Carry it today: Name three ordinary goods on your table tomorrow, out loud.

StoicismWiki ↗

What is Stoicism? A philosophy founded by Zeno of Citium around 300 BC in Athens, named for the painted porch (stoa) where he taught. Its most famous teachers are Epictetus, born into slavery; Seneca, a Roman statesman; and the emperor Marcus Aurelius. The Stoics taught that we can't control events, only our judgments and choices. Their four virtues are wisdom, courage, justice and self-control.

"Adapt thyself to the things with which thy lot has been cast: and the men among whom thou hast received thy portion, love them, but do it truly, sincerely." (Marcus Aurelius, Meditations 6.39, Long translation.) On Thanksgiving eve, Marcus gives good table advice: these are the people your lot has given you. Love them truly. The Stoic version of gratitude is accepting your portion, and the people in it, with a whole heart.

Today's readings through Hindu and Jain eyes: the harvest offering.

  • The Hindu lens: Food is offered to God as prasad before it is eaten, a daily thanksgiving.
  • The Jain lens: Jains eat before sunset and give thanks for food gathered with the least harm.

Today is Krishna Pratipada, the first day after the full moon, with the moon moving from Rohini into Mrigashira (per Indian calendars; exact Chicago times couldn't be confirmed). In northern India's calendar the month of Margashirsha begins today. Kanaka Dasa Jayanti is Friday.

What the words mean (all Sanskrit):

  • Pratipada: "the first day."
  • Rohini: "the red one."
  • Prasad: "grace," food offered to God.
  • Margashirsha: named for the deer's-head star, Mrigashira.

The time. Jeremiah prophesied in Jerusalem from about 627 to 587 BC, through its fall to Babylon. John was written around AD 90–100.

What scholars broadly agree on. Jeremiah 30–33, the "Book of Consolation," gathers the prophet's hope-filled words; some may have been shaped by later editors during the exile.

What it meant then. For a people who had lost their land, Temple and king, it promised a return and a full harvest.

The allegorical readings. The fathers saw the gathered flock as the church gathered from all nations, and the watered garden as the soul watered by grace.

What's debated or unknown. Which parts of chapters 30–33 come from Jeremiah himself.
