November 28th, 2026The five-minute version

The readings: Micah opens with people who lie awake at night scheming, then get up and take the fields and houses of their neighbors (paraphrased). God says he is planning too: their scheme will fall. But then Micah turns. God will gather the scattered remnant like sheep in a fold. One who breaks open the way will go before them; they'll break through the gate and go out, their king ahead of them, the Lord at their head (paraphrased). In Matthew, Jesus says his coming will be as plain as lightning across the sky (paraphrased).

What it means: On the last day of the church year, the image is a shepherd who doesn't just gather the flock but breaks the wall open and walks out ahead of them. Advent begins tomorrow with that hope: God makes a way where there wasn't one.

Across the traditions: the same truth, in other words.

  • Judaism: Today is Shabbat, a weekly rest from grabbing and getting.
  • Bahá'í: Tonight Bahá'ís mark the Ascension of 'Abdu'l-Bahá, who called himself the servant of all.
  • Islam: The Qur'an warns, do not consume one another's wealth unjustly (2:188, paraphrased).
  • Buddhism: The second precept is not to take what is not given.

Carry it today: What wall needs breaking through?

The theme

  • Schemes at night: Woe to the land-grabbers.
  • Sheep in a fold: The remnant gathered.
  • The breaker: One who opens the way.
  • Lightning: The Son of Man's coming.

Yesterday, Zechariah promised light at evening. Today, Micah promises a shepherd who breaks the gate open.

1. Plotting in Bed

Micah warns those who lie in bed devising wickedness, and at morning light carry it out, coveting fields and seizing them, houses and taking them (paraphrased).

  • Land theft: In Israel, land was a family's inheritance from God; stealing it destroyed a family.
  • God's counterplan: God says he too is devising something, against them.

2. Don't Preach Like That

Micah's hearers tell him to stop preaching such things (paraphrased). He answers that a preacher of wine and strong drink would suit them better.

  • Unwelcome words: Prophets weren't popular.
  • Easy comfort: People prefer a message that asks nothing.

3. Sheep in a Fold

God promises to gather all Jacob, the survivors of Israel, like sheep in a fold, like a flock in its pasture, noisy with people (paraphrased).

  • Shepherd again: The image that has run through this whole week.
  • A crowd: The remnant turns out to be many.

4. The One Who Breaks Out

The one who breaks open the way will go up before them; they will break through and pass the gate, their king before them, the Lord at their head (paraphrased).

  • The breaker: In Hebrew, ha-poretz, the one who breaks through.
  • Advent hope: Christians have read this as the Messiah going ahead of his people.

5. Like Lightning

Jesus warns against false messiahs and says the coming of the Son of Man will be like lightning that shines from east to west; he will send his angels with a trumpet to gather his chosen from the four winds (paraphrased). The gathering Micah promised becomes worldwide.

For Christians today

The church year ends today; tomorrow, it begins again.

  • Catholic: The year's last Mass reading is the last chapter of the Bible, the river of life; the psalm prays, "Marana tha! Come, Lord Jesus!"
  • Episcopal: Kamehameha IV and Emma translated the Book of Common Prayer into Hawaiian and founded Queen's Hospital in Honolulu.
  • Orthodox: St. Stephen the New died for the right to venerate icons, the faces of the saints.
  • Protestant and evangelical: Many families light the first Advent candle tomorrow, the candle of hope.
  • Rest: Many Christians use this weekend to set up an Advent wreath or calendar.

Carry it today: Set out an Advent candle for tomorrow and name one way you hope God will break through.

Today's readings through Hindu and Jain eyes: not grasping.

  • The Hindu lens: The Isha Upanishad says to enjoy the world by renouncing it, and not covet anyone's wealth (verse 1, paraphrased).
  • The Jain lens: Aparigraha, non-possessiveness, is one of the five great Jain vows.

Today is Krishna Panchami, the fifth day after the full moon, with the moon moving from Punarvasu into Pushya (per Indian calendars; exact Chicago times couldn't be confirmed). Month: Margashirsha (northern reckoning).

What the words mean (all Sanskrit):

  • Aparigraha: "non-grasping."
  • Panchami: "the fifth."
  • Pushya: "nourishing," the most auspicious of the lunar stars.

StoicismWiki ↗

What is Stoicism? A philosophy founded by Zeno of Citium around 300 BC in Athens, named for the painted porch (stoa) where he taught. Its most famous teachers are Epictetus, born into slavery; Seneca, a Roman statesman; and the emperor Marcus Aurelius. The Stoics taught that we can't control events, only our judgments and choices. Their four virtues are wisdom, courage, justice and self-control.

Epictetus taught that wealth is not in having many possessions but in having few wants. (Discourses, paraphrased.) Micah's land-grabbers lay awake wanting more. The Stoic answer is to want less, and sleep well.

The time. Micah prophesied around 740–700 BC, in the time of Isaiah, as Assyria threatened Israel and Judah. Matthew was written around AD 80–90.

What scholars broadly agree on. Micah spoke for small farmers against wealthy landowners. Many scholars think the hopeful verses 2:12–13 were added or placed later, possibly during the exile.

What it meant then. For dispossessed farmers, it was God siding with them. For exiles, it promised a way home.

The allegorical readings. The fathers read "the breaker" as Christ, who broke the gates of death and leads his people out.

What's debated or unknown. Whether 2:12–13 is Micah's own word of hope or a later addition, and whether it was first a promise or a threat (some read the "fold" as a siege).
