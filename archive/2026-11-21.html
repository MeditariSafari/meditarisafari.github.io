November 21st, 2026The five-minute version

The readings: Ezekiel has just blasted Israel's leaders as shepherds who fed themselves and let the sheep scatter. Now God promises to do the shepherding himself: a covenant of peace, wild animals gone, rain in its season, a flock that lives in safety. "You are my sheep, the sheep of my pasture, and I am your God." In Matthew, Jesus is teaching a crowd when someone tells him his mother and brothers are outside. He asks, "Who is my mother, and who are my brothers?" Then he points to his disciples: "Here are my mother and my brothers!" Anyone who does God's will is family.

What it means: Jesus isn't rejecting his mother; she'll be at the cross. He's opening the door. The family of God isn't defined by blood, and the shepherd's flock is bigger than anyone thought.

Across the traditions: the same truth, in other words.

  • Epictetus: "Never say of anything, 'I have lost it'; but, 'I have returned it.'" (Enchiridion 11.) Even family is ours on loan.
  • Judaism: On this Shabbat, Jacob dreams of a ladder and wakes saying God was in this place and he didn't know it.
  • Sikhism: Guru Nanak taught that all people are one family under one Creator.
  • Islam: The Qur'an calls believers brothers and sisters to one another (49:10, paraphrased).

Carry it today: Who is family to you beyond blood?

Yesterday, God came like a shepherd carrying the lambs. Today, the shepherd says the whole flock is family.

1. A Covenant of Peace

God promises to make a covenant of peace with his sheep, banish the wild animals, and send showers of blessing in their season (Ezekiel 34:25–26, paraphrased).

  • Shalom: Peace here means more than no war: safety, plenty, wholeness.
  • Sleeping in the woods: The flock can rest even in the wild places.

2. No Longer Prey

The people will live securely, no longer plundered by other nations or devoured by wild beasts, and no one will make them afraid (Ezekiel 34:28, paraphrased).

  • After exile: For refugees in Babylon, this was everything.
  • The bad shepherds: Earlier in the chapter, God condemns leaders who ate the fat and clothed themselves with the wool but didn't care for the weak.

3. The Sheep of My Pasture

"You are my sheep, the sheep of my pasture, and I am your God, says the Lord God."

  • Belonging: The whole promise ends in a relationship.
  • Psalm 100 echo: "We are his people and the sheep of his pasture."

4. Who Is My Mother?

"Who is my mother, and who are my brothers?"

  • Outside: His family stood outside the house; the disciples sat inside with him.
  • Earlier worry: Mark says his family thought he'd lost his mind (Mark 3:21).

5. Here Are My Mother and My Brothers

"Here are my mother and my brothers! For whoever does the will of my Father in heaven is my brother and sister and mother." Jesus adds "sister" when he widens the circle; the crowd may not have expected women in that family. Today the Catholic and Orthodox churches honor Mary herself, who first said yes to God's will. She is family twice over: by blood, and by doing the will of God.

For Christians today

The question "who is my family?" runs through Christian life.

  • The oldest readings: Augustine said Mary was more blessed for doing God's will than for giving birth to Christ.
  • Catholic and Orthodox: Today's feast remembers Mary as a child given to God in the Temple, a model of the disciple who belongs wholly to God.
  • Episcopal: Mechthild and Gertrude, today's commemoration, were nuns who wrote of the Sacred Heart of Jesus as a home for all.
  • Protestant and evangelical: Many churches call one another "brother" and "sister" because of this passage.
  • Live debates today: Some read it as a call to welcome the lonely, single, estranged and LGBTQ believers into church as real family.

Carry it today: Treat someone who isn't related to you like family.

StoicismWiki ↗

What is Stoicism? A philosophy founded by Zeno of Citium around 300 BC in Athens, named for the painted porch (stoa) where he taught. Its most famous teachers are Epictetus, born into slavery; Seneca, a Roman statesman; and the emperor Marcus Aurelius. The Stoics taught that we can't control events, only our judgments and choices. Their four virtues are wisdom, courage, justice and self-control.

"Never say of anything, 'I have lost it'; but, 'I have returned it.'" (Epictetus, Enchiridion 11, Higginson translation.) Epictetus applies this even to a child or a spouse: they were given, and they go back. The Stoics also taught that every human being is a fellow citizen of one world-city. Jesus pointing to his disciples says something similar: family isn't a possession, and it's far wider than blood.

The time. Ezekiel prophesied in Babylon among the exiles, about 593–571 BC. Matthew was written around AD 80–90.

What scholars broadly agree on. Ancient Near Eastern kings were commonly called shepherds, so Ezekiel 34 is political: Israel's kings had failed, and God would take their place.

What it meant then. For Jesus' hearers, family was everything: your identity, your safety, your inheritance. Redefining it around God's will was radical.

The allegorical readings. The fathers read the "covenant of peace" as the new covenant in Christ, and the good shepherd of Ezekiel 34 as Jesus (John 10).

What's debated or unknown. Who Jesus' "brothers" were: Catholics and Orthodox say cousins or Joseph's sons by an earlier marriage; most Protestants say Mary's later children.
