“Three women save a baby Pharaoh wants dead: his mother hides him, his sister watches over him, and Pharaoh's own daughter takes pity and adopts him.”
November 17th, 2026The five-minute version

The readings: The lectionary jumps back to Exodus. Pharaoh has ordered every Hebrew boy thrown into the Nile. One mother sees that her son is "a fine baby" and hides him for three months. When she can't hide him any longer, she makes a little basket, waterproofs it, and sets him among the reeds. His sister watches from a distance. Pharaoh's daughter comes to bathe, opens the basket, and the baby is crying. "She took pity on him." The sister steps forward and offers to find a Hebrew nurse, and brings the baby's own mother. She is paid to raise her own son. Pharaoh's daughter names him Moses: "I drew him out of the water."

What it means: The whole story of Israel's freedom begins with a baby's cry and a woman's pity. Pharaoh had the power, but three women, one of them his own daughter, quietly undid his decree. Pity isn't weakness. It's where rescue starts.

Across the traditions: the same truth, in other words.

  • Marcus Aurelius: "Consider that a good disposition is invincible, if it be genuine." (Meditations 11.18, Long.)
  • Judaism: The rabbis named Pharaoh's daughter Bitya, "daughter of God," because she saved a child who wasn't hers (Leviticus Rabbah 1:3, paraphrased).
  • Buddha: He taught compassion as wishing that all beings be free from suffering (paraphrased).
  • Islam: The Qur'an tells how God inspired Moses' mother to set him on the river, and how Pharaoh's wife said, don't kill him (28:7–9, paraphrased).

Carry it today: Whose cry will move you to act today?

The theme

  • A mother hides him: Three months of courage.
  • A basket on the Nile: Faith set afloat.
  • A sister watches: From a distance.
  • Pharaoh's daughter: Pity across every line.

Yesterday, Jael, an outsider, ended a war. Today, another outsider, Pharaoh's own daughter, saves the boy who will free Israel.

1. A Fine Baby

"The woman conceived and bore a son, and when she saw that he was a fine baby, she hid him three months."

  • "Fine": The Hebrew word is tov, "good," the same word God uses for creation in Genesis 1.
  • Civil disobedience: Like the midwives before her (Exodus 1:17), she defies Pharaoh.

2. A Papyrus Basket

"When she could hide him no longer she got a papyrus basket for him and plastered it with bitumen and pitch; she put the child in it and placed it among the reeds on the bank of the river."

  • An ark: The Hebrew word for basket, tevah, is the same word used for Noah's ark.
  • Among the reeds: The same Hebrew word as the Red (Reed) Sea he'll later cross.

3. His Sister Watched

"His sister stood at a distance, to see what would happen to him."

  • Miriam: Tradition names her as Miriam, later a prophet.
  • Quick thinking: She offers to find a nurse, and brings their mother.

4. She Took Pity

"When she opened it, she saw the child. He was crying, and she took pity on him. 'This must be one of the Hebrews' children,' she said."

  • She knew: She saw it was a Hebrew baby, and saved him anyway.
  • Against her father: Her compassion overrode the king's law.

5. Drawn Out of the Water

"When the child grew up, she brought him to Pharaoh's daughter, and he became her son. She named him Moses, 'because,' she said, 'I drew him out of the water.'" The one drawn out of the water will one day draw his people through the water. Paul's advice fits the women of this story: "Encourage the fainthearted, help the weak, be patient with all of them." None of them was powerful. All of them were brave.

For Christians today

The rescue of Moses echoes through the Christian story.

  • The oldest readings: The fathers saw the baby in the basket as a figure of Christ, hidden from Herod's slaughter of the infants (Matthew 2).
  • Catholic: Today's saint, Elizabeth of Hungary, a king's daughter like Pharaoh's daughter, gave away her wealth and built a hospital before dying at 24.
  • Protestant and evangelical: Moses' mother is often held up as an example of faith that takes risks (Hebrews 11:23).
  • Paul's advice: "Rejoice always, pray without ceasing, give thanks in all circumstances" (1 Thessalonians 5:16–18).
  • Live debates today: The story of a baby saved from a government's decree is often cited in debates about refugees and immigrant children.

Carry it today: Answer one cry for help you might have walked past.

StoicismWiki ↗

What is Stoicism? A philosophy founded by Zeno of Citium around 300 BC in Athens, named for the painted porch (stoa) where he taught. Its most famous teachers are Epictetus, born into slavery; Seneca, a Roman statesman; and the emperor Marcus Aurelius. The Stoics taught that we can't control events, only our judgments and choices. Their four virtues are wisdom, courage, justice and self-control.

"Ninth, consider that a good disposition is invincible, if it be genuine, and not an affected smile and acting a part." (Marcus Aurelius, Meditations 11.18, Long translation.) Pharaoh had armies; his daughter had pity. Marcus, an emperor himself, knew which was stronger. Real kindness, not a performance of it, can't be defeated, and it undid a king's decree.

The time. Exodus is set in Egypt, traditionally in the 13th century BC. 1 Thessalonians was written around AD 50.

What scholars broadly agree on. The name Moses is probably Egyptian, meaning "son" or "born of" (as in Thutmose, Ramesses); the Hebrew explanation, "drawn out," is a wordplay. A similar story was told of Sargon of Akkad, set afloat in a basket as a baby.

What it meant then. For Israel, the story showed God protecting the deliverer from the start, through the courage of ordinary women.

The allegorical readings. The fathers read the basket on the water as baptism, and Pharaoh's daughter as the church of the Gentiles adopting Christ.

What's debated or unknown. Which Pharaoh, and when, is unknown; there is no Egyptian record of Moses.
