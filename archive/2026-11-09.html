November 9th, 2026The five-minute version

The readings: The book of Joshua ends at Shechem. Joshua writes the covenant into the book of the law, then sets up a large stone under the oak by the sanctuary. "This stone shall be a witness," he says, "for it has heard all the words of the Lord." Then Joshua dies, at 110. The people serve God for as long as Joshua and the elders who knew him are alive. And one more burial: the bones of Joseph, carried out of Egypt through forty years of wilderness, are finally laid to rest at Shechem, in the field Jacob bought centuries earlier. A long story comes home.

What it means: Joshua knows he won't be there forever, so he leaves a witness: a stone that "heard" the promise. The people stay faithful as long as those who remembered are alive. Faith is passed on by people who saw, and by things that remind us. On a day when Catholics celebrate a church building and Ethiopian Jews celebrate the renewal of the covenant, the question is: what witnesses to our promises after we're gone?

Across the traditions: the same truth, in other words.

  • Marcus Aurelius: "Everything is only for a day, both that which remembers and that which is remembered." (Meditations 4.35, Long.)
  • Judaism: Today Ethiopian Jews keep Sigd, a festival renewing the covenant, recalling Ezra reading the Torah to the returned exiles.
  • Buddha: He taught that all conditioned things pass away (Digha Nikaya 16, paraphrased).
  • Islam: The Qur'an says everything on earth will pass, but the face of your Lord remains (55:26–27, paraphrased).

Carry it today: What will witness to your promises after you're gone?

The theme

  • The covenant sealed: Joshua writes it down and sets up a stone.
  • A stone that heard: The witness to Israel's promise.
  • Joshua dies: The servant of the Lord, at 110.
  • Joseph comes home: Bones carried for forty years are finally buried.

For weeks the lectionary followed Moses, then Joshua. Today Joshua's story ends, and with it the long journey from Egypt to the land.

1. A Covenant at Shechem

"So Joshua made a covenant with the people that day and made statutes and ordinances for them at Shechem."

  • Written down: "Joshua wrote these words in the book of the law of God."
  • At Shechem: Where Abraham first heard God's promise in the land.

2. A Stone That Heard

"See, this stone shall be a witness against us, for it has heard all the words of the Lord that he spoke to us."

  • Under the oak: The same place, perhaps, where Jacob buried his household's idols (Genesis 35:4).
  • A silent witness: The stone will still stand when everyone present is gone.

3. The Servant of the Lord

"After these things Joshua son of Nun, the servant of the Lord, died, being one hundred ten years old."

  • A title of honor: Joshua began as "Moses' assistant." He dies with Moses' own title, "servant of the Lord."
  • 110 years: The same age as Joseph at his death (Genesis 50:26).

4. While the Witnesses Lived

"Israel served the Lord all the days of Joshua and all the days of the elders who outlived Joshua."

  • Living memory: Faith held as long as people remembered firsthand.
  • A warning: The book of Judges begins with a generation "who did not know the Lord" (Judges 2:10).

5. Joseph Comes Home

"The bones of Joseph, which the Israelites had brought up from Egypt, were buried at Shechem." Joseph had asked, centuries before, that his bones be carried out when God brought Israel home (Genesis 50:25). Moses took them out of Egypt (Exodus 13:19). Through forty years in the wilderness and every battle since, someone carried those bones. Now the promise is kept. The whole story, from Abraham's first altar at Shechem to Joseph's burial there, comes full circle. Some promises take generations to keep, and someone has to carry them the whole way.

For Christians today

The end of Joshua speaks to how faith is passed on.

  • The oldest readings: The fathers saw Joshua, whose name is the Hebrew form of "Jesus," as a figure of Christ, who leads us into the promised rest (Hebrews 4:8).
  • Catholic: Today's feast honors the Lateran Basilica, dedicated in 324, but its readings point beyond buildings: "Do you not know that you are God's temple?" (1 Corinthians 3:16).
  • Protestant and evangelical: Joshua's charge and the stone of witness inspire covenant renewal services, such as John Wesley's Covenant Service.
  • Hebrews 11:22: Joseph's request about his bones is listed among the great acts of faith.
  • Live debates today: How churches pass faith to a generation that didn't see what their elders saw is one of the great questions of our time.

Carry it today: Tell someone younger one thing God has done in your life.

Today's readings through a Buddhist lens: impermanence and remembrance.

  • The last words: The Buddha's final words were that all conditioned things pass away; strive on with heedfulness (Digha Nikaya 16, paraphrased).
  • Stupas: After his death, the Buddha's relics were placed in stupas, monuments of remembrance, much like Joshua's stone.

StoicismWiki ↗

What is Stoicism? A philosophy founded by Zeno of Citium around 300 BC in Athens, named for the painted porch (stoa) where he taught. Its most famous teachers are Epictetus, born into slavery; Seneca, a Roman statesman; and the emperor Marcus Aurelius. The Stoics taught that we can't control events, only our judgments and choices. Their four virtues are wisdom, courage, justice and self-control.

"Everything is only for a day, both that which remembers and that which is remembered." (Marcus Aurelius, Meditations 4.35, Long translation.) Marcus reminds himself that both memory and the remembered pass. Joshua knew it too, which is why he set up a stone. The Stoic answer is to live well now; the biblical answer adds a witness that outlasts us, and a God who doesn't forget.

The time. Joshua is set in the late 13th century BC. 1 Corinthians was written around AD 54.

What scholars broadly agree on. Joshua 24 likely preserves an ancient covenant tradition at Shechem. A large standing stone excavated at Shechem (Tell Balata) has been connected by some with this story. Joseph's tomb is traditionally located near Nablus, ancient Shechem.

What it meant then. For later Israel, the book's ending set the standard: faithfulness depended on remembering.

The allegorical readings. The fathers saw the stone of witness as Christ, the living stone.

What's debated or unknown. Whether Joseph's traditional tomb near Nablus is the original site is debated.
