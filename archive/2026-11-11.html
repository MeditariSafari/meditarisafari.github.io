“God promises a new covenant, written not on stone but on the heart. Jesus says his words will outlast heaven and earth.”
November 11th, 2026The five-minute version

The readings: Jeremiah writes as Jerusalem falls. The old covenant, carved on stone at Sinai, has been broken over and over. But God isn't finished. "The days are surely coming," says the Lord, when there will be a new covenant. This time the law won't be on tablets; God will write it on their hearts. No one will need to teach their neighbor to know God, "for they shall all know me, from the least of them to the greatest." And God will forgive, and remember their sin no more. In Matthew, Jesus says everything we see will pass away, "but my words will not pass away."

What it means: Rules written outside us can be read, argued with and broken. A law written on the heart becomes who we are. Jeremiah's hope is that God will change us from the inside. Christians hear in it the promise Jesus spoke of at the Last Supper: "the new covenant in my blood."

Across the traditions: the same truth, in other words.

  • Epictetus: "Some things are in our control and others not." (Enchiridion 1.) What's truly ours is within.
  • Judaism: Jeremiah's new covenant is read as God's promise to renew Israel's heart, still awaited.
  • Buddha: He taught that the mind is the forerunner of all things (Dhammapada 1, paraphrased).
  • Islam: The Qur'an says God has written faith upon the hearts of believers (58:22, paraphrased).

Carry it today: What is written on your heart, not just on paper?

The theme

  • A new covenant: God's promise after the old one was broken.
  • Written within: Not on stone, but on hearts.
  • All shall know me: From the least to the greatest.
  • Words that last: Heaven and earth pass away; his words don't.

Monday, Joshua set up a stone of witness. Tuesday, Ezra read the law from a scroll. Today, Jeremiah promises a law no stone or scroll can hold.

1. The Days Are Surely Coming

"The days are surely coming, says the Lord, when I will make a new covenant with the house of Israel and the house of Judah."

  • Hope in the ruins: Jeremiah speaks this as Babylon besieges Jerusalem.
  • The only "new covenant": This is the only place the Hebrew Bible uses the phrase.

2. A Covenant They Broke

"It will not be like the covenant that I made with their ancestors when I took them by the hand to bring them out of the land of Egypt—a covenant that they broke, though I was their husband, says the Lord."

  • Taken by the hand: God led them like a parent leads a child.
  • Like a marriage: The prophets often call the covenant a marriage, and the people's unfaithfulness adultery.

3. On Their Hearts

"I will put my law within them, and I will write it on their hearts, and I will be their God, and they shall be my people."

  • Inside, not outside: The law becomes desire, not just duty.
  • The same promise: "I will be their God, and they shall be my people," the heart of every covenant.

4. From the Least to the Greatest

"For they shall all know me, from the least of them to the greatest, says the Lord."

  • No gatekeepers: Knowing God won't depend on rank or learning.
  • To know: In Hebrew, "know" means intimate relationship, not just information.

5. Remember Their Sin No More

"For I will forgive their iniquity and remember their sin no more." The whole new covenant rests on forgiveness. God doesn't just write a better law; God wipes the slate clean so the writing can begin. Hebrews quotes this passage in full (Hebrews 8:8–12), the longest Old Testament quotation in the New Testament. And Jesus' words, "Heaven and earth will pass away, but my words will not pass away," mean this promise will outlast everything.

For Christians today

The new covenant is at the center of Christian faith.

  • The oldest readings: At the Last Supper, Jesus took the cup and called it "the new covenant in my blood" (Luke 22:20). The very name "New Testament" means "new covenant."
  • Catholic: Today's saint, Martin of Tours, was a Roman soldier who cut his cloak in half for a freezing beggar, then dreamed Christ was wearing it. He later refused to fight, saying he was a soldier of Christ. The law written on his heart moved his hands.
  • Protestant and evangelical: The Reformers stressed that the Spirit writes God's law on the heart, so that we obey from love, not fear.
  • Veterans Day: Armistice Day began as a day to remember the dead of World War I and to pray for lasting peace. Martin, the soldier saint, shares its date.
  • Live debates today: How the new covenant relates to God's covenant with the Jewish people is a central and sensitive question; many churches now affirm that God's covenant with Israel was never revoked (Romans 11:29).

Carry it today: Thank a veteran, and pray for peace.

StoicismWiki ↗

What is Stoicism? A philosophy founded by Zeno of Citium around 300 BC in Athens, named for the painted porch (stoa) where he taught. Its most famous teachers are Epictetus, born into slavery; Seneca, a Roman statesman; and the emperor Marcus Aurelius. The Stoics taught that we can't control events, only our judgments and choices. Their four virtues are wisdom, courage, justice and self-control.

"Some things are in our control and others not. Things in our control are opinion, pursuit, desire, aversion." (Epictetus, Enchiridion 1.) Epictetus, born a slave, found that what is truly ours lies inside: how we judge, what we desire, what we turn from. Jeremiah's promise goes further: God reaches into that very place, the heart's desires, and writes there.

The time. Jeremiah prophesied from about 627 BC until after Jerusalem fell in 587 BC. Matthew was written around AD 80–90.

What scholars broadly agree on. Jeremiah 30–31, the "Book of Consolation," gathers words of hope for the exiles. Many scholars think the new covenant passage was shaped by Jeremiah's followers during the exile.

What it meant then. For exiles who had lost Temple, land and king, the promise meant that God's covenant could survive anywhere, carried inside them.

The allegorical readings. The fathers saw the tablets of stone as the old law and the heart of flesh (Ezekiel 36:26) as the Spirit's work in the church.

What's debated or unknown. Exactly what "this generation will not pass away" (Matthew 24:34) refers to is one of the most debated verses in the Gospels.
