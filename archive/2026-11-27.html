November 27th, 2026The five-minute version

The readings: The church year is almost over, and the readings look to the end of all things. Zechariah sees a strange day, neither day nor night, when at evening time there will be light. Living water flows out of Jerusalem both ways, in summer and in winter, and the Lord becomes king over all the earth (paraphrased). Psalm 80 prays, "Restore us, O God; let your face shine." Paul writes to a young church grieving its dead: don't grieve as those without hope; we will be with the Lord forever; encourage one another (paraphrased).

What it means: Evening is when light usually leaves. Zechariah promises the reverse: light that arrives just when you expect the dark. In the shortest days of the year, two days before Advent, that's the hope Christians keep.

Across the traditions: the same truth, in other words.

  • Judaism: Tonight Jews light Shabbat candles as the sun sets, light at evening (Chicago: 4:03 PM).
  • Islam: Friday is Jumu'ah, the day of congregational prayer.
  • Bahá'í: Tomorrow Bahá'ís mark the Ascension of 'Abdu'l-Bahá, keeping a vigil in the small hours.
  • Seneca: He urged that each evening we be able to say, I have lived. (Letters 12, paraphrased.)

Carry it today: Where are you waiting for light?

Yesterday, the psalmist asked God's face to shine. Today, Zechariah promises light where we least expect it.

1. The Day of the Lord

Zechariah sees the nations gathered against Jerusalem, and God standing on the Mount of Olives, which splits in two (paraphrased).

  • The end of history: The prophets' "day of the Lord" is when God sets everything right.
  • Mount of Olives: The same hill where, in Acts, Jesus ascends and the angels promise he will return.

2. Light at Evening

Zechariah says there will be one continuous day, known to the Lord, neither day nor night, and at evening time there will be light (Zechariah 14:7, paraphrased).

  • A reversal: Evening is when darkness falls; here light rises.
  • Advent hope: The church will light its first candle on Sunday.

3. Living Water

On that day living water will flow out from Jerusalem, half to the eastern sea and half to the western, in summer and in winter (paraphrased).

  • Never dries up: Most streams in Judea run dry in summer; this one never does.
  • Echoes: Ezekiel's river from the Temple, and Jesus' promise of living water in John 4.

4. One Lord, One Name

The Lord will become king over all the earth; on that day the Lord will be one and his name one (Zechariah 14:9, paraphrased).

  • The Shema: It echoes Israel's great prayer, that the Lord is one (Deuteronomy 6:4, paraphrased).
  • Christ the King: Last Sunday's feast, still echoing at week's end.

5. Not Without Hope

Paul tells the Thessalonians not to grieve like those who have no hope, because those who have died in Christ will rise, and we will be with the Lord forever. Therefore, he says, encourage one another (1 Thessalonians 4:18, paraphrased). After a holiday with empty chairs, that's a word for today.

For Christians today

The last days of the church year point to the end and the beginning.

  • Catholic: Today's Gospel says the fig tree's new leaves show summer is near; Revelation shows a new heaven and a new earth.
  • Episcopal: Morning Prayer reads Jesus' entry into Jerusalem, a reading that returns on Palm Sunday and in some traditions on Advent Sunday.
  • Orthodox: The Nativity Fast, begun November 15, continues toward Christmas.
  • Protestant and evangelical: Many churches are hanging Advent wreaths this weekend.
  • Funerals: 1 Thessalonians 4 is one of the most common readings at Christian funerals.

Carry it today: Before dark tonight, light a candle and name one hope.

StoicismWiki ↗

What is Stoicism? A philosophy founded by Zeno of Citium around 300 BC in Athens, named for the painted porch (stoa) where he taught. Its most famous teachers are Epictetus, born into slavery; Seneca, a Roman statesman; and the emperor Marcus Aurelius. The Stoics taught that we can't control events, only our judgments and choices. Their four virtues are wisdom, courage, justice and self-control.

Seneca wrote that we should go to sleep each night content, saying, I have lived, I have run the course fortune gave me; if God adds tomorrow, receive it gladly. (Letters to Lucilius 12, paraphrased.) On the last days of the church year, Seneca's evening practice fits. Treat today as complete, and tomorrow as a gift.

The time. Zechariah 9–14 is usually dated later than chapters 1–8 (about 520 BC), perhaps the 5th or 4th century BC. 1 Thessalonians was written around AD 50, likely the oldest book in the New Testament.

What scholars broadly agree on. Zechariah 14 is apocalyptic: it uses vivid images for God's final victory. Paul wrote 1 Thessalonians from Corinth to a young church he had left in a hurry.

What it meant then. For a small, struggling Jewish community, Zechariah promised that God's reign would reach the whole earth. For the Thessalonians, Paul answered a painful question: what about believers who die before Christ returns?

The allegorical readings. The fathers read the living water as baptism and the Spirit, and the light at evening as Christ, who came in the evening of the ages.

What's debated or unknown. Who wrote Zechariah 9–14, and how literally Paul expected the events of 1 Thessalonians 4.
