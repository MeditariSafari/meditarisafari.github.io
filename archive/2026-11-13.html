“God keeps raising up judges to rescue Israel, but each time a judge dies, the people fall back, worse than before.”
November 13th, 2026The five-minute version

The readings: Judges lays out its pattern. Israel forgets God and falls under enemies. God hears their groaning and raises up a judge, a leader who rescues them. As long as the judge lives, things go well. "But whenever the judge died, they would relapse and behave worse than their ancestors." Then the cycle starts again. In Revelation, even as the bowls of judgment fall, the people "did not repent." And in the middle, a word of hope: "Blessed is the one who stays awake."

What it means: Faith that depends on one strong leader collapses when the leader is gone. Israel was faithful while someone was watching. The question Judges leaves us is whether our faithfulness is our own, or borrowed from whoever is holding us up.

Across the traditions: the same truth, in other words.

  • Epictetus: "How long, then, will you put off thinking yourself worthy of the highest improvements?" (Enchiridion 50.)
  • Judaism: Tonight Shabbat begins, a weekly practice that keeps faith from depending on any one leader.
  • Buddha: He told his followers to be a lamp unto themselves (Digha Nikaya 16, paraphrased).
  • Islam: The Qur'an says God does not change a people's condition until they change what is in themselves (13:11, paraphrased).

Carry it today: What keeps you faithful when no one is watching?

The theme

  • Relapse: Worse than before.

This week the story has moved from Joshua's death, to the generation that forgot, and now to the cycle that runs through the rest of the book.

1. God Raises Up Judges

"Then the Lord raised up judges who delivered them out of the power of those who plundered them."

  • Not courtroom judges: These were rescuers and tribal leaders: Deborah, Gideon, Samson.
  • God's initiative: Each rescue starts with God, not with Israel.

2. They Did Not Listen

"Yet they did not listen even to their judges, for they lusted after other gods and bowed down to them."

  • Strong language: The Hebrew word suggests unfaithfulness, like adultery.
  • Rescue isn't change: Being saved didn't change their hearts.

3. The Lord Was With the Judge

"Whenever the Lord raised up judges for them, the Lord was with the judge."

  • God's compassion: The verse goes on to say God was moved by their groaning.
  • Borrowed strength: The people leaned on the judge's faith.

4. Worse Than Their Ancestors

"But whenever the judge died, they would relapse and behave worse than their ancestors."

  • A downward spiral: Each relapse goes deeper.
  • The book's ending: By the last chapters, "all the people did what was right in their own eyes" (Judges 21:25, paraphrased).

5. Stay Awake

Revelation's word in the middle of judgment is "Blessed is the one who stays awake." Judges shows what happens when faith sleeps between rescuers. The answer, as Jeremiah promised on Wednesday, isn't another strong leader but a changed heart, a faith that stays awake on its own. Christians believe the final Judge, Christ, doesn't die and leave us to relapse.

For Christians today

The cycle of Judges is familiar to anyone who has fallen back after a fresh start.

  • The oldest readings: The fathers saw in the judges figures of Christ, the deliverer who doesn't die and leave his people.
  • Catholic: Today's saint, Frances Cabrini, founded schools, orphanages and hospitals for Italian immigrants across the Americas, a faith that kept working when no one was watching.
  • Orthodox: St. John Chrysostom, honored today, was famous for preaching that true worship includes caring for the poor.
  • Recovery: Many in recovery know the pattern: rescue, a season of strength, relapse. The answer, as in Judges, is daily faithfulness, not one heroic moment.
  • Live debates today: How much churches rely on charismatic leaders, and what happens when those leaders fall or leave, is a pressing question in many communities.

Carry it today: Do one faithful thing that no one will see.

StoicismWiki ↗

What is Stoicism? A philosophy founded by Zeno of Citium around 300 BC in Athens, named for the painted porch (stoa) where he taught. Its most famous teachers are Epictetus, born into slavery; Seneca, a Roman statesman; and the emperor Marcus Aurelius. The Stoics taught that we can't control events, only our judgments and choices. Their four virtues are wisdom, courage, justice and self-control.

"How long, then, will you put off thinking yourself worthy of the highest improvements?" (Epictetus, Enchiridion 50.) Epictetus tells his students to stop waiting for a teacher to make them good. You're no longer a child; start now. Israel kept waiting for the next judge. Both say: don't borrow your virtue from someone else.

The time. Judges is set in the 12th and 11th centuries BC. Revelation was written around AD 95.

What scholars broadly agree on. The judges were local heroes whose stories were gathered and framed by later editors in the repeating pattern of sin, oppression, cry, rescue.

What it meant then. For later Israel, the cycle explained why the nation needed lasting faithfulness, and for some, a king.

The allegorical readings. The fathers read the judges' victories as Christ's victories over sin and death.

What's debated or unknown. Whether the judges ruled one after another or overlapped in different regions is debated.
